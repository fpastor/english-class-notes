## Parts of a successful project (1/2)

**The Team**

The success of projects depends on teamwork. The team leader should support team members, and every individual needs to be a good team player. You can:
- lead / head (up) / run... a team
- build / form... a team
- join a team
- work together as a team
- team up with somebody = Work together with them.
- be a team leader / team member / team player
  
Projects have a **schedule** (time plan) and a **deadline** (final date). You can:
- fall behind schedule and then catch up
- be on schedule
- finish ahead of schedule
- agree / confirm / set / extend... a deadline
- meet / miss... a deadline

## Parts of a successful project (2/2)

**The Bugdet**

Projects also need a **budget** (money available to spend). Before the project starts you can:
- estimate (or underestimate) costs
- make a rough (approximate) estimate of costs
  
And once it is running you should try to:  
- keep within the budget
- stick to the budget (also stick to the schedule)

## Suggestions (1/2)

When we decline to do something, in general, we suggest an alternative, because we want to be polite.

So, it's common for us to say:
- **How about** / **What about** working on the ceiling instead?
- We **could** go and buy the paint.
- **Why don't** I / we work on the painting?
- **Let's** finish the back room. (More Informal)

## Suggestions (2/2)

When we make a suggestion or recommendation in English we say:
  
**I suggest..., I propose..., I recommend...**
- Are you **suggesting** we change plans? (We might clarify something.)
- We'd like to **propose** a different solution. (We might signal we are going to talk about something.)
- They **recommend** a change to the plans. (We might report what someone has said.)
